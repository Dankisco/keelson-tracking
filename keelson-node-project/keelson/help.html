Tracking

What a Keelson tracking number looks like

Three letters and nine digits, always beginning KLN — for example KLN482910375. It appears on your booking confirmation. Spaces and hyphens are ignored, so you can paste it in however it was sent to you.

My tracking page hasn’t changed in days

That is normal on long sea legs. A container crossing the Pacific may go ten days between checkpoints, because there is nothing to report until it reaches the next port. The estimated arrival date is the figure to watch, not the number of updates.

The arrival date moved

Estimated arrival changes when a vessel misses a berth window, a customs entry takes longer than usual, or weather reroutes a leg. When it moves, the reason is written onto the checkpoint that caused it.

Who else can see my shipment

Anyone holding the tracking number can see the tracking page. That is deliberate — it is how you share progress with a consignee without giving them an account. The page shows the route, the status and the checkpoints. It does not show your account details, the freight rate you were quoted, or any other shipment. Treat the number as you would a parcel reference: useful to share, not a secret, and not a password.

Customs

Why it waits

Customs authorities decide the order and speed of clearance, not the carrier. A shipment sitting at Customs clearance has been lodged correctly and is queued. Random inspections add days rather than hours, and no carrier can shorten them.

Duties and taxes

The freight rate you were quoted covers carriage only. Import duty, VAT and port charges are assessed at the destination and billed separately. Who pays depends on the incoterm on your booking.

What slows clearance most

An invoice value that does not match the goods, a missing certificate of origin, or a commodity code that contradicts the description. If we spot one of these we raise an Exception and contact the booking account rather than letting the entry fail.

What we cannot carry

{{ COMPANY.legal_name or 'Keelson' }} does not accept goods prohibited by the carrier, the origin country or the destination country. Dangerous goods need to be declared at booking, not on collection. Ask the operations desk before booking anything you are unsure about.

Problems and claims

Before you call

Open the tracking page and read the most recent checkpoint. It names the location and, where there is one, the reason for a hold. That answers most queries on its own.

Raising it with us

  1. Send the tracking number to the operations desk.
  2. We trace it with the carrier or terminal handling that leg.
  3. You get a written answer with a revised arrival date, or the reason one cannot be given yet.

Damage, shortage or loss

Avoiding fake tracking links

Freight attracts fraud, and convincing copies of carrier tracking pages are common. Four things are always true of us.

  • Check the web address before you type anything into it. {% if COMPANY.domain %}Our only address is {{ COMPANY.domain }}. {% endif %}A lookalike spelling, a different ending or a link shortener is not us.
  • We never ask for a payment to release a shipment. No genuine Keelson message will ask you to pay a fee to free cargo, clear customs or avoid storage charges.
  • We never ask for your password, card or bank details by email or message. Not for verification, not for a refund, not ever.
  • A tracking number cannot change anything. It shows progress. It cannot redirect a shipment, alter an address or release cargo.

If a message looks wrong, do not click it. Type the tracking number into the tracking box on our own site instead, and tell the operations desk what you received.
